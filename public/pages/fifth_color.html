The Fifth Color

A game of topological entrapment by yours truly

Rules

Materials

A blank sheet of paper or other drawable material.

Four distinct colored markers(e.g., red, green, blue, and yellow) OR 4 clearly predetermined symbols(e.g, 1, 2, 3, and 4).

A writing utensil.

Starting

To start off the game, draw one closed area on the paper. Do not color it in.

Turns

Part 1

Color in the uncolored, empty area using one of the four colors. You cannot use a color that matches an area sharing a boundary edge(corners do not count) with the current empty area. e.g., if player 1 draws an area connected to a red area, player 2 cannot use red to color in that area. Note that the first player always colors in the starting area. A player loses if, at the start of Part 1 of their turn, they color the uncolored area with a fifth color. This usually happens because it cannot be colored with any of the four colors.

Part 2

Draw one, new, empty area on the paper. Do not color it in. The new area must be drawn outside any existing areas. You cannot place an area inside of another area. The new area must share at least one boundary edge(corners, again, do not count) with an existing colored area(No unconnected areas except for the starting one. All areas must be connected to all other areas).

Explanation

This game was inspired by the four color theorem, which states that it is impossible to create a map that cannot be colored with 4 colors while following the rules of adjacency. While the normal 4 color theorem would imply that this game is impossible to lose, note that in this version of the game, you cannot erase your previous colorings. This is what makes the game possible to lose, as humans will eventually make a mistake or plan badly enough to allow their opponent to trap them.
